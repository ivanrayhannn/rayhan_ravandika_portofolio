Logistics at Toyota Indonesia: milkrun and crossdock

As a Fujitsu consultant dedicated to Toyota Indonesia's logistics, I work on the systems behind how parts move from suppliers to the plant. This page explains two of those flows, milkrun and crossdock, in general terms, and where a business system analyst fits in.

Milkrun: one truck, many suppliers

Transport is paid per resource: every truck and trip costs money. So suppliers in the same zone are grouped into one route, and one truck collects from all of them in a loop on a fixed schedule, instead of one truck per supplier.

  1. PlanGroup suppliers by zone, one route per zone, and a time window for each supplier.
  2. OrderPR, then PO to each supplier, then the parts to collect on each cycle.
  3. Pick upThe truck visits the suppliers in a set order.
  4. ArriveAt TMMIN, inside its time window.
  5. ReturnEmpty packaging goes back to the suppliers on the next loop.

Crossdock: one pack, one destination

Crossdock is a separate flow from milkrun. Here the supplier packs parts for one destination, following a packing instruction that is generated automatically from part availability and TMMIN's orders.

  1. OrderTMMIN's orders say which parts each destination needs.
  2. AvailabilityThe system checks which parts are available.
  3. InstructionA packing instruction is generated automatically for each supplier.
  4. PackThe supplier packs the parts for one destination.
  5. ShipEach pack goes to its destination.

Where systems help

The two flows are separate, but both run on timing and accuracy. This is the kind of support a logistics system gives each step, and where requirements work starts.
